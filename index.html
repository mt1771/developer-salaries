Do U.S. Developers Really Earn Double?
Michael Taglia

The Myth:

U.S. devs earn twice Germany's

Verdict:

It's complicated

At every experience level, U.S. developers report higher salaries
Share of developers who use AI tools
What I found and why it matters

The myth holds up on paper: U.S. developers report a median salary of about $145k, roughly double Germany’s $75k and well above Japan’s $59k and China’s $46k. The gap appears at every experience level. The trend lines put a brand new U.S. developer at about $128k, compared with about $57k in Germany, and U.S. salaries stay on top across the full range of experience. Interestingly, the highest paid country reports the lowest AI tool use: about 58% of U.S. and German developers use AI tools, compared with 66% in Japan and 88% in China.

How I know and what it does not tell us

This analysis uses 6,094 developers from the Stack Overflow 2024 Developer Survey who reported a salary: 4,168 in the U.S., 1,766 in Germany, 94 in Japan and 66 in China. A Welch’s ANOVA found that mean salaries differ significantly across the four countries (p < 0.001). Games-Howell post-hoc comparisons found significant differences between every pair of countries except Japan and China (adjusted p = 0.50). A MANOVA testing salary and experience together also found significant differences across countries (Pillai’s trace = 0.25, p < 0.001). In follow-up tests, salary showed a substantially stronger effect than experience (F = 651.5 vs. 38.7). The trend lines and AI chart are descriptive and were not formally tested.

Limitations: Salaries are converted to U.S. dollars with no adjustment for cost of living, so a higher salary does not necessarily mean a higher standard of living. Respondents also chose to take the survey, and China (66) and Japan (94) have small samples, so their results are less reliable.
